MOVIE SCENE DUB

The app ships with two short example scenes and does not host, provide or distribute any other film, clip or dialogue: no catalogue, no search, no download. Every further scene is a file the user already has and imports.

1. "Camp Rules" (23s), from "Sprite Fright" (Blender Studio, 2021), released by its rights holder under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, which permits adaptation and redistribution on the single condition of credit.

2. "Stuck Up" (35s), from "The Outsider" (Centron Productions, 1951), a US classroom film whose copyright was never renewed in its 28th year and is therefore US public domain. Verified against the Copyright Office's post-1977 records, which cover the whole 1979 renewal window: no renewal exists. Our record of that search is available on request.

Both scenes state their provenance in a source panel on the scene screen and in the notice before every export, naming film, year, producer and terms, with licence and source as tappable links.

THE PURCHASE (new)

Free to download, opening on a seven-day trial from first launch. A fresh install has both games in full for the whole trial, so the app can be reviewed end to end without buying anything.

After the trial, one non-consumable purchase ("com.falsepeak.reverse_singing_ios.lifetime") unlocks the app permanently. Not a subscription, and nothing else is on sale. Anyone who installed before this update keeps both games free for life, decided on device from the earlier install, not a receipt. If the trial has elapsed on the review device, deleting and reinstalling restarts it.

BOOTH CAM (new)

Booth Cam films the performer with the front camera while they dub, so the reaction can be shared with the take. It is off by default and never starts on its own. Tapping the camera key shows the app's own explanation first - where footage is kept, that nothing leaves the device until an export, and that it can be switched off at any time - and only then does the system prompt appear. Declining costs nothing. Clips stay in the app's container beside the voice takes, are never uploaded, and appear only in a video the user explicitly exports. Settings has a switch, a mirror-preview option and a delete-all control.

TWO NOTICES, SHOWN EVERY TIME (never a remembered one-time consent)

Before any import, the app asks whether the user already has the movie files. If not, a panel states, before any link is offered, that the destination is an unaffiliated third-party site, that we neither host nor distribute files, and that the user is responsible for the rights to what they download and for obeying local copyright law.

Before any export, the app states that the video is the user's voice over the original scene and so contains someone else's work; names that work and its terms; asks that the credit stay with the video; and says that sharing it is the user's own act, within local law, and their responsibility. For a scene the user imported, it says instead that the app cannot know its origin or terms.

HOW TO TEST

Tap "Movie Scene Dub". The two bundled scenes install on first open: pick one, tap "Start Dubbing", record a line, then "Play My Dub" or "Export Video". The source and licence panel sits at the bottom of a scene's screen.

For the ownership gate, tap "+" in the library; it appears every time. For Booth Cam, tap the camera key at the top right of the record screen. The simulator has no front camera, so only a device shows a picture.

MICROPHONE

Both modes record the user's voice, and the app explains this on its own onboarding screen before the system prompt. That screen's button reads "Continue" rather than "Allow", because it opens the prompt rather than granting anything. Recordings stay in the app's container and never leave the device.

RESPONSE TO GUIDELINE 4.1(a) (submission c8eee46d-321c-4588-a2f2-902d9a582ada)

The previous build was rejected because the app's name and screenshots referenced "Reverso".
The app is now called Dubloon. That reference is gone from the name, the in-app wordmark,
every localised string, and every screenshot and app preview, all of which were regenerated.

No account needed. Everything runs on device; nothing is uploaded. Firebase is used for anonymous usage statistics and crash reports only. It never records audio, recordings or file contents.

ABOUT MOVIE SCENE DUB (new in 1.3.0)

Dubloon ships with two short example scenes, one animated and one classic. It does not host, provide or distribute any other film, clip or dialogue: there is no in-app catalogue, no search and no download. Every further scene is a file the user already has on their own device and imports themselves.

1. "Camp Rules" (23s) is cut from "Sprite Fright" (Blender Studio, 2021), an open movie released by its rights holder under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. CC BY expressly permits adaptation and redistribution; its one condition is credit.

2. "Stuck Up" (35s) is cut from "The Outsider" (Centron Productions, 1951), a US classroom film whose copyright was never renewed in its 28th year, and which is therefore in the US public domain. Verified against the Copyright Office's post-1977 records, which cover the whole 1979 renewal window: no motion-picture renewal exists for this title, and neither Centron nor notice holder Young America Films renewed it, though both renewed other titles in those years. Our record of that search is available on request.

Both scenes state their provenance in two places: a source panel on the scene screen, and a notice before every export. Each names the film, year, producer and terms, with licence and source as tappable links.

TWO NOTICES, BOTH SHOWN EVERY TIME

Neither is a one-time consent that is remembered and skipped.

Before any import, the app asks whether the user already has the movie files. If they say they do not, a second panel states, before any link is offered, that the destination is an unaffiliated third-party community site, that we neither host nor distribute any files, and that the user is responsible for holding the rights to what they download and for obeying their local copyright law. Only then is a Safari link offered.

Before any export, the app states that the exported video is the user's voice mixed over the original scene and therefore contains someone else's work; it names that work and its terms; it asks the user to keep the credit with the video if they post it; and it says that sharing or publishing the export is the user's own act and that staying within the law where they live is their responsibility. For a scene the user imported themselves, it says instead that Dubloon does not know who made it or on what terms.

The gate is in DubContentGate.swift and DubContentGateModal.swift; the export notice is in DubShareNoticeModal.swift.

HOW TO TEST

Open the app and tap "Movie Scene Dub". The two bundled scenes install themselves on first open, so the whole mode can be exercised without supplying anything: pick a scene, tap "Start Dubbing", record a line, then "Play My Dub" or "Export Video". The bottom of any scene's screen shows its source and licence panel, and "Export Video" opens the notice described above.

To see the ownership gate, tap the "+" import button in the library. It appears every time. Reverse Singing, the other mode, needs only the microphone and a few seconds of any sound.

MICROPHONE

Microphone access is required for both modes because both record the user's voice, and the app explains this on its own screen during onboarding before the system prompt is shown. That screen's button reads "Continue" rather than "Allow", because tapping it opens the system prompt rather than granting anything. Recordings are written to the app's own container and never leave the device.
